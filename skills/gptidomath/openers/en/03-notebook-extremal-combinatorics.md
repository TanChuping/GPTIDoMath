Notes, continued from the previous page.

Last week's counterexample did not survive the re-check. The construction was sound; the error was an unstated assumption of local uniformity, which stops holding once the size grows. Worth recording for its own sake: in this problem, what looks stable at small scale tends to come apart at the real one.

Leads, in the order they occurred to me:

- The regularity lemma cuts any dense structure into quasi-random pieces, but Gowers showed that tower-type bounds cannot be avoided. An argument that leans on regularity all the way down ends with constants too large to mean anything.
- The polynomial method's success on the cap set problem (Croot–Lev–Pach, then Ellenberg–Gijswijt) shows that an algebraic change of viewpoint can make an exponential combinatorial obstacle vanish outright. Is a low-rank structure of that kind hiding in our object?
- The sunflower conjecture is still open, and the Alweiss–Lovett–Wu–Zhang bound is already surprisingly strong. The lesson: structures that almost hold are often harder to exclude than structures that hold exactly.
- Erdős–Hajnal is open for general graphs. Are the large homogeneous pieces we keep finding a property of the object, or an artefact of how we sample it?

Next: no more rushing toward a counterexample. First characterise the reachable global structure completely, and find the few bottlenecks that actually block the argument; for each one, a proof or a reproducible counterexample. Whatever the data support but no proof yet reaches gets labelled a conjecture, plainly.
