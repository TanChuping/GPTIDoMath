**Stability of spectral gaps in finite quantum many-body systems (working abstract)**

*Background.* Lieb–Robinson bounds give local interactions a finite speed of propagation. Hastings proved the entanglement area law for gapped one-dimensional systems; in two dimensions it is open. The Haldane gap of the spin-1 Heisenberg chain is beyond numerical doubt, yet rigorous proofs reach only solvable points such as the AKLT model. A deeper limit is due to Cubitt, Pérez-García and Wolf: for two-dimensional translation-invariant nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians the spectral gap problem is undecidable. The quantum PCP conjecture is unresolved, although the NLTS theorem has been proved.

*Questions.* Consider a finite system assembled from many local terms, each well behaved on its own.

1. Under what conditions do local perturbations leave the global gap open? Which hypotheses of a Bravyi–Hastings–Michalakis-type stability argument are still unmet here?
2. Can the correlations of the low-energy states be captured faithfully by a tensor network of controlled size? If not, where exactly is the obstruction?
3. Which local constraints that look unrelated combine into degeneracy or frustration that appears only at full size?

*Method.* What can be proved is proved. Where a proof is out of reach, the evidence is numerical and reproducible, and it states which proposition it supports.
