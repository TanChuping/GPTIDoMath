Mathematical research session · opening note (the continuation of a long-running project)

This is a mathematics problem, and an old one for us. It lives in directed algebraic topology, where the execution spaces of concurrent processes become d-spaces and every schedule is a directed path. There, deadlocks and unreachable regions are holes carved out by forbidden zones, and two schedules are "the same" only if a directed homotopy connects them. We are also studying the space through persistent homology along a natural filtration, using discrete Morse theory to collapse it onto its critical cells, and running a spectral sequence that assembles many local computations into a global answer. And there is the obstruction-theoretic heart of the matter: local schedules that are pairwise compatible on every overlap, which still refuse to glue into one global section, as if a Čech class with coefficients in the sheaf of admissible schedules were quietly nonzero.

The object looks humble: a finite configuration space generated by many asynchronous components and the constraints between them. Its topology is not humble. Features persist across every scale we try. Holes vanish under one coarsening and reappear under another. Coherence conditions hold strictly in each piece but only up to homotopy when the pieces are composed. The goal is to compute the invariants that actually matter, to show which obstructions are real and which are artefacts of the chosen cover, and to find the configuration that is optimal under hard resource and ordering constraints.

Half of the work is proof: exhibiting the directed path that realises a bad state, or proving that no such path exists in any reachable component. The other half is experimental mathematics: every claim is checked against measurements of the real system before it counts, and anything unverified stays a conjecture.
